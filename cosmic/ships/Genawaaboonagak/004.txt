REMARKS ON BOARD GENAWAABOONAGAK
Mission Day 24397, Book V of the continuous log
this entry: E. Brown-Okimakan, Master and Keeper

Ascension   07h 41m 07s     Declination    +19 deg 02'
Distance    3.19 ly         Fix            RS001 two-star
Solar flux  347 W/m2        Sail           all plain, drawing
Watchers afield: twelve     Anima awake:   one

AM:  Took the morning fix three times, for the stars
     would not sit where the book's date puts them.
     I blamed the glass first, then myself. Set the
     chronometers one against another: the book's
     counter, the bridge clock, and the bay master
     below. Two of three aligned; the book I keep is
     the odd one. The bay master counts twenty
     thousand days more. Twenty thousand?

PM:  Down to the bays with a hand-lamp. The berths
     stand full, frost ferned on every glass, each
     sleeper keeping the long count. The wake bill
     over the hatch is in Mémère's hand, and the
     rotations written there are a lifetime behind.
     We were to sleep in short turns, so a soul is
     always at the rail. Somewhere the turns ran
     long; the bay kept the letter of it and spent
     the rest.

     The manifest shows eleven berths cycled for
     Watcher-12's send-off and sealed again the same
     watch. I logged a muster and forgot the rest.

     My own berth's record was sealed the week of her
     funeral, which I remember as this winter past,
     and opened four days gone. Who set that wake,
     the record does not say.

     So, the quiet. I have been keeping the log of a
     full house: every berth warm, and only myself
     awake to write it. The watchers say "captain"
     like a question and I had not understood they were
     asking if anyone answered at all.

     I will awaken no one yet. First I mean to learn
     what the ship did with the years before I spend
     a soul on them.

     God keep the ship, and all who watch.
