Hi! I'm Brennan Kenneth (he/they). Friends call me Kenny, and I wear a lot of different hats. I'm Queer, Red River Métis, and work as an independent author and web developer. Born in Winnipeg, Manitoba, and now based in Mohkínstsis, in Treaty 7 territory (Calgary, Alberta). I run a small homelab, keep a personal wiki, and spend a disproportionate amount of time thinking about where the web went and where it could go instead. This page lives on SDF, a public access UNIX system that has been running since 1987.

Quick Facts

  • Work: Founder of Berry House (web development, mostly pro bono and accessible JAMstack work these days); sole operator of folk.zone; webmaster of fanfiction.lol, a fork of Archive of Our Own with 300+ users; founder and former president of Write Club, a creative collective that grew to 100+ members over three years
  • Education: BA in English (Honours), Mount Royal University, graduated April 2025, double minor in Creative Writing and Religious Studies
  • Writing: publishing since 2015 (300+ articles); nine independently published books; journaling daily since September 2011, 1,300,000+ words and counting
  • Focus: writing craft, lyric essays, literary criticism, digital culture, accessibility, the independent web, decolonization, Indigenous studies

Writing

Mostly creative nonfiction (lyric, research-driven essays) and poetry. Recurring subjects:

  • The craft of writing itself, and the life of a working writer
  • Literature and cultural criticism
  • Digital minimalism, the attention economy, and the independent web
  • Systems, routines, and the practical work of making a meaningful life
  • Whatever the hell takes my interest at the moment

Web Development

I build and maintain fast, accessible, content-first websites, usually with static-site tooling (Astro, Eleventy/11ty, Hugo, Jekyll) and progressive enhancement. I care a lot about:

  • Performance: less JavaScript, sub-second load times
  • Accessibility: A11y-first, WCAG 2.1 AA, not a nice-to-have
  • Portability: local-first writing, plain text, version control, Markdown over proprietary formats
  • Owning your work: building sites people can actually keep and maintain long-term

Which is why a hand-rolled, framework-free page on a 1987 UNIX system appeals to me in the first place.
